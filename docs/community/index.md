# Relationships, Partnerships, and Community

At the core of any community engaged endeavor are relationships. Deep and meaningful relationships are the foundation for solid partnerships. Too often, the nature of community engaged work is characterized by a "drop-in, drop-out" approach where the academic comes in, does their thing or gets what they need, and leaves to go on to their next thing. Even the most well-intentioned researchers are prone to fall into this practice because the academic system is built on it. For early career scholars and non-tenure track faculty, there are particular challenges to building long-term relationships and partnerships that arise as many postitions are only funded for two years or come with other requirements and don't leave ample time to devote to the work. Community engaged research is slow, deep work, and it requires a foundation that takes time and intentionality to build, but this labor is often not recognized or valued by institutions as necessary to the process.

Non-extractive partnerships: community engaged research is not charity work or a form of "giving back"; it is a synthesis of expertise oriented toward a shared goal or outcome

## *Putting Knowledge into Practice: Communiversities as Education Without Walls*

*The Communiversities Grand Research Challenge project, led by Drs. Asif Wilson (University of Illinois Urbana Champaign), Rachel McMillian (Indiana University Indianapolis), and Aja Reynolds (Wayne State University), is a multi-sited collaborative initiative that convenes communities to critically read and rewrite their worlds in three Midwestern cities:  Chicago, Cincinnati, and Detroit. Communiversities builds on a model from the 1960s that emerged out of the Black Power movement in Chicago where parents, teachers, professors, and community organizers came together to create alternative education sites to imagine and actualize freedom.

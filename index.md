# Community Engaged Research Toolkit Home
## Reciprocal and Redistributive Community Engaged Research

All research has a code of ethics behind it and all research prioritizes some things over others, whether that is outcome or process or the timeline or something else. This varies from field to field and project, but the more explicit we are about those underlying priorities and ethical underpinnings, the more transparent the research process becomes for all involved. A part of this transparency is understanding the relationship between methods and ethics in your project and determining how they shape one another. 

In community-engaged research, replicability is not exact. You can’t copy-paste methods from one project to the next in local contexts while also accounting for the specific nature, character, needs, and wants of that particular community. We carry pieces what we learn from each other from one endeavor to the next and apply pieces to the projects where they fit, allowing the methods to adapt and grow across contexts.

In the context of reciprocal and redistributive methods, collaboration is more than two parties working together toward a shared outcome. Collaborations rooted in reciprocity prioritize redistributing power, resources, and benefits, with long term impacts in mind.
